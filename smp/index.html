    Getting started

    ObbyTrap is a survival server with an economy run by its players. You earn shards from jobs, skills and quests, open crates with the keys they give you, and trade with everyone else.

    Joining

    • Address: obbytrap.net
    • The server runs Minecraft 1.21.11. Older and newer versions can connect too, but that one plays best.
    • You start with no shards and no items. Your first kit and your first job fix that.
    • Raiding, Griefing and PvP are allowed and encouraged.

    Your first ten minutes

    1. Type /kits and take the Trapped kit: iron tools, iron armour, a shield and food.
    2. Type /rtp and pick the overworld to land somewhere of your own.
    3. Type /sethome home where you want to live, so /home home brings you back.
    4. Type /jobs and shift-click up to three jobs that match what you like doing. See Jobs.
    5. Type /quests and pick a first quest to work towards.
    6. After 30 minutes, type /rewards for the daily reward and your first playtime level.

    Every day

    • Claim /rewards, and check your playtime levels and server levels.
    • Do the daily quest of each of your jobs with /jobs quests.
    • Open the crate keys you have earned.

    Finding your way

    • /guide (or /help) opens the server guide in game, with a button for each part of the server.
    • At spawn, each character opens one thing when you click it: the shop, the auction house, orders, kits, jobs, skills, quests, teams, vaults, rewards, warps, player warps, bounties, the blood money shop and random teleport.
    • /spawn takes you back there.
    • The boards at spawn rank the top ten players by shards, coins, playtime, kills, deaths, and blocks broken and placed.

    Typing commands Press Tab while typing a command to see the commands you can use and to finish the one you have started.

    The shop

    /shop opens the server shop: seven categories of items at fixed prices, paid in shards.

    • In a category, left-click an item to buy it and right-click to sell it. Shift and right-click sells every one you are carrying.
    • /sell hand sells the stack in your hand, /sell handall every stack of that item, and /sell all everything in your inventory the shop buys.
    • If two categories buy the same item, /sell all pays the higher price.
    • Prices are for one item. The shop does not work in Adventure or Spectator mode.

    Selling pays far less than buying costs Most items sell for a tenth of their buying price or less, so buying to sell back always loses money. Sugar cane and cactus are the exceptions that make farming worth it: they sell for $250 and $110 each.

    Trading with players

    Four ways to trade: the auction house, buy orders, paying someone directly, and shops at player warps.

    Auction house

    • /ah opens it. /ah sell <price> lists the item in your hand, and /ah sell <price> <amount> lists part of the stack.
    • The lowest price you can ask is $10. There is no highest price, no listing fee and no tax: the seller gets the full price.
    • A listing stays up for 24 hours. After that it moves to your expired items, where you have 7 days to take it back before it is deleted.
    • /ah search <text> finds items, /ah view shows your own listings and /ah history what you have bought and sold.
    • Buying asks you to confirm first. Everyone sees a line above their hotbar when something new is listed.

    Orders

    /orders is the auction house the other way round: you say what you want and what you will pay, and other players deliver it.

    • Pick the item, the amount and the price for each one. The lowest price is $1 each.
    • You can have 10 orders open at once.
    • Anyone can open your order and hand over the items. They are paid straight away, and you collect the items from Your orders.
    • When an order ends or you cancel it, you get your shards back for whatever was not delivered. An order can't be cancelled while delivered items are still waiting for you to collect.
    • Orders can be sorted by most paid, most delivered, newest and most money per item, and filtered by kind of item.

    Paying and player shops

    • /pay <player> <amount> sends shards. Nothing is taken off.
    • /points pay <player> <amount> sends coins.
    • /settings has a switch to stop other players paying you.
    • Players run their own shops at player warps: /pw lists them, and the Shop category holds the shops.

    Coin flip

    A 50/50 bet against another player, in shards.

    • /cf opens the list of open flips. Create one and type your bet in chat, or click someone else's to take it. Joining asks you to confirm.
    • The smallest bet is $500 and the largest $1,000,000.
    • The winner takes both bets less 20%. Two $5,000 bets make a $10,000 pot, and the winner receives $8,000.
    • You can close a flip you created while it is still waiting for an opponent. Once someone has joined it runs to the end.
    • The menu keeps your wins, losses and totals.

    Jobs

    A job pays you for things you would be doing anyway. You can hold three at once, and each one runs to level 48 with a reward to claim at every level.

    • /jobs opens the jobs menu. Shift-click a job to join it, and shift-click it again to leave. A plain click opens that job's level rewards.
    • Every paid action gives shards, job points and job experience. Pay shows above your hotbar as you work.
    • /jobs payouts <job> lists everything a job pays for.

    How pay works

    • Pay rises 1% with every job level. At level 48 an action pays about 46% more than it did at level 2.
    • Each extra job you hold lowers the pay of all of them by 1%. With three jobs, each pays 2% less.
    • There is no hourly limit on what a job can pay.
    • Nothing is paid in Creative mode, for blocks near a spawner, or for exploring while flying or gliding.
    • Breaking a block that a player placed doesn't pay.
    • Placing ore as a Miner costs you shards, and so does placing bamboo, sugar cane, cactus or vines as a Farmer.
    • Jobs count 5 furnaces, 2 blast furnaces, 2 smokers and 10 brewing stands for each player. Filling them with hoppers doesn't pay.

    Levels and their rewards

    • Reaching level 48 takes about 1.8 million job experience. The whole server is told when someone gets there.
    • Leaving a job and coming back to it later costs 30% of your level, unless you had reached level 48.
    • /jobs <job> opens the job's rewards. Each level has one, and you claim it by clicking it once you have reached that level.
    • Every level pays shards, from about $100 at level 1 to about $54,000 at level 48. All 48 together come to about $700,000 for each job.
    • Most levels add something else: job points, experience, or items that fit the job, such as enchanted books, tools and armour.

    Skills

    Skills level up on their own as you mine, fight, fish and farm. Higher levels unlock abilities, and every 100 levels in a skill unlocks a reward.

    • /skills opens the skills menu: your profile, a page for each skill, milestones and the boost shop.
    • The skills are Mining, Woodcutting, Excavation, Herbalism, Fishing, Swords, Axes, Archery, Unarmed, Taming, Acrobatics, Alchemy, Repair, Salvage and Smelting.
    • Skill levels have no upper limit. Each level needs a little more experience than the last.
    • Your power level is all your skill levels added together. Server levels need it.
    • /mcstats lists your levels and /mctop the best players.
    • Mobs from spawners and spawn eggs give no skill experience.

    Abilities

    • The big ones unlock at level 50 of their skill: Super Breaker (Mining), Tree Feller (Woodcutting), Giga Drill Breaker (Excavation), Green Terra (Herbalism), Serrated Strikes (Swords), Skull Splitter (Axes) and Berserk (Unarmed).
    • Right-click with the tool to ready one, then use the tool. Each has a 4 minute cooldown and lasts one second longer for every 50 levels.
    • Blast Mining unlocks at Mining 100 and has a 1 minute cooldown.
    • Repair works on an iron block and Salvage on a gold block. Both ask you to click twice.
    • /mcability turns abilities off and on for you.

    Boost shop

    • /mcmmo shop sells experience boosts for one skill at a time. They are paid for with your own experience points, not shards.
    • Each boost lasts 60 minutes. Buying the same one again adds another 60.
    • The seven boosts cost 100,000 experience points for 1.1x, 150,000 for 1.25x, 200,000 for 1.5x, 250,000 for 2x, 300,000 for 2.5x, 350,000 for 3x and 400,000 for 4x.

    Parties

    • /party create <name> starts a party for sharing skill experience. A party has no size limit, and members can't hurt each other.
    • A party levels up as its members do, to level 10. Party chat comes at level 1, /ptp teleport at 2, alliances at 5, item sharing at 8 and experience sharing at 10.
    • Shared experience reaches members within 75 blocks and comes with a bonus, from 10% up to 50% as more members are nearby.
    • /ptp <player> has a 2 minute cooldown, a 5 second wait, and the other player has to accept.
    • A member who has not been online for 7 days is removed from the party.

    Parties and teams are different things A party shares skill experience. A team shares a home, warps and a chest.

    Quests

    More than 1,600 quests, sorted into pools such as mining, hunting, farming and fishing. Most come in chains: finish one to unlock the next.

    • /quests opens the pools. A quest shows its task, its reward and its difficulty: easy, medium or hard.
    • A locked quest is waiting for the one before it in its chain.
    • Quests don't count progress in Creative mode.
    • Each pool has a top ten. You need three finished quests in a pool to appear on it.

    Crates and keys

    • Left-click a crate to see what is in it, with the chance of each reward. Right-click with a key to open it. Sneak and right-click to open up to 30 at once.
    • Keys are items: tripwire hooks with a glow. They can't be placed or crafted.
    • The sign above each crate shows how many keys you have, how many opens are left until your bonus key, and the last winner.
    • Rewards are common, rare, epic or legendary. Epic and legendary wins are announced to the server.

    Blood money

    Blood money is the killer's currency. The only way to make it is to kill other players, and the more of it you carry, the more your own head is worth.

    • /bm shows your blood money, the bounty on you, your kills and deaths, and what your head would pay.
    • /bm shop opens the shop, /bm top the richest killers and /bm wanted the biggest bounties.
    • /bm stats shows what you have earned, lost and laundered.
    • Amounts can be typed as 500, 2.5k or all.

    What a kill pays

    PartAmount
    New blood money50, plus 5% of what the victim was carrying, up to 5,000
    Taken from the victim10% of their blood money, if they had 100 or more
    Their bountyAll of it, up to 10,000, plus any contracts on them
    • A kill made with TNT, a respawn anchor or an end crystal counts for whoever placed it, for two hours after it was placed.
    • If nobody lands the last hit, the kill goes to a player who hurt the victim in the 30 seconds before.

    What stops farming

    • Killing the same player again within two hours pays half, then a quarter, then a tenth, then nothing.
    • A player who keeps dying pays less each time within an hour: three quarters, half, a quarter, then nothing.
    • A victim with under 30 minutes of playtime creates no new blood money. A killer with under 30 minutes gets only the 10%.
    • Kills between accounts on the same connection pay nothing.
    • One killer can make 20,000 new blood money in an hour and 60,000 in a day.

    The bounty on you

    • Once you carry 1,000 blood money, a bounty starts to build on you by itself.
    • It grows by 0.5% of your blood money an hour while you are out in the world and moving, and faster the longer you hold on to it, up to three times that rate.
    • It stops at 40% of what you carry, or 25,000, whichever is lower. It doesn't grow while you are offline or standing idle.
    • Whoever kills you is paid it, and it starts again from nothing.
    • /bm bounty shows who wants you dead and for how much.

    Contracts

    • /bm contract <player> <amount> puts your blood money on someone's head. Whoever kills them collects it.
    • A contract is between 250 and 25,000. Placing one costs 5% on top.
    • You can have 5 contracts out at once, worth 50,000 in total.
    • A contract lasts 7 days. If nobody collects, you get the amount back.
    • The target must have played for 2 hours and been online in the last 7 days. You can't collect your own contract.
    • /bm contracts lists the ones that are open.

    Laundering

    • /bm launder <amount> turns blood money into shards at 25%: 1,000 blood money becomes $250.
    • Blood money earned in the last 6 hours launders worse, starting at half that rate.
    • The least you can launder is 100. There is a 10 minute wait between launders and a daily limit of 20,000.
    • You have 30 seconds to confirm. Laundering and contracts are blocked for 15 seconds after a fight.

    Bounties

    A bounty is shards on someone's head. It is separate from blood money, and both can pay out on the same kill.

    • /bounty opens the list of bounties, biggest first.
    • /bounty add <player> <amount> places one. You are asked to confirm, the amount is a whole number, and you can't place one on yourself.
    • Whoever kills that player is paid the whole bounty in shards.
    • New bounties and collected ones are announced to the server. The bounty menu lets you turn those announcements off for yourself.

    Getting around

    Random teleport to find land, homes to get back to it, and requests to reach your friends.

    Random teleport

    • /rtp opens a menu with three choices: the overworld, the nether and the end.
    • You land somewhere safe within 1,000 blocks of the middle of that world.
    • Stand still for 5 seconds after choosing, or it is cancelled. It is free.
    • You can use it once every 10 minutes, whichever world you pick.

    Homes

    • /sethome <name> saves the spot you are standing on, and /home <name> takes you back. /delhome and /renamehome tidy them up.
    • Homes and /spawn have no wait and no cooldown. You can't be hurt for 4 seconds after any teleport.
    • Setting a home with a name you have already used replaces the old one without asking.
    • With no home set, /home takes you to spawn.
    • When you die you wake up at your bed, if you have slept in one.

    Other players and warps

    • /tpa <player> asks to go to someone, and /tpahere <player> asks them to come to you. They answer with /tpaccept or /tpdeny.
    • A request lasts 2 minutes. /settings has a switch to stop requests reaching you.
    • /warps opens the server's own places: the arena, the crates, the leaderboards and the AFK area.
    • Joining, and teleports over a distance, play a short fly-in from above. You can't chat or type commands until it ends.
    • After 5 minutes without moving you are marked as away. Nobody is kicked for it.

    Player warps

    Warps that players make for their own shops, farms and builds. Anyone can visit them.

    • /pw opens them: your favourites, every warp, the ones you visited lately, and eight categories (Building, Shop, Farm, PvP, Event, Fun, Special and Other).
    • Left-click a warp to go there after a 3 second wait. Right-click it to rate it from 1 to 5 stars or add it to your favourites.
    • The list can be sorted by visits, rating, favourites, distance, age or name.
    • Making a warp is free. A name is 1 to 16 letters with no spaces, and a description is up to 3 lines.
    • In My warps, right-click one of yours to change its name, icon, description or category, set a price for visiting, or delete it.
    • An owner can keep a warp to a list of invited players, or ban players from it.

    Teams

    A team is up to 8 players with a shared home, two warps, a chest and a chat of their own. Making one is free.

    • /team create <name> starts a team. The name is 4 to 10 letters.
    • /team invite send <player> invites someone. They answer with /team invite accept or /team invite deny.
    • /team sethome sets the team home and /team home goes to it: a 3 second wait, and 2 minutes between uses.
    • A team can keep 2 warps. /team warp <name> goes to one, with the same wait and cooldown.
    • /team echest opens the team chest, three rows that every member shares.
    • Team mates can't hurt each other until the leader turns that on with /team pvp.
    • /team ally add <leader> makes an ally, up to 4, and /team enemy add <leader> an enemy, up to 2. /allychat <message> talks to your allies.
    • /team prefix <text> sets the 3 to 8 letter tag members wear.
    • The leader can /team promote, /team demote, /team kick and /team transfer. /team disband asks you to confirm.
    • /team info and /team list show teams, /team leave leaves yours, and /teams opens a page of all these commands.

    • /pv opens your vaults. Click one you own to open it, or a locked one to go to the vault shop.

    Chat and settings

    • Type [item] in chat to show what you are holding, [inv] your inventory, [ec] your ender chest, [shulker] the shulker box in your hand and [playtime] your time played.
    • Writing @name pings that player with a sound and a line on their screen.
    • Click a name in chat to start a private message to them with /msg.
    • You can send one message every 3 seconds, up to 80 characters. Links, addresses and phone numbers are blocked.
    • /settings has switches for teleport requests, payments from other players, private messages and the scoreboard.

    Rules and help
